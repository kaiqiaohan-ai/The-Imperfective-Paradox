# The Imperfective Paradox Is Not Necessarily in Large Language Models: A Benchmark Failure Before a Model Failure

This repository is for our [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25005). The current release contains the **Lexically Matched Minimal Pairs** dataset. Additional resources may be added in future updates.

## Dataset: Lexically Matched Minimal Pairs

The dataset provides a revised, controlled evaluation setting that addresses the benchmark-design problems identified in the paper: classifying aspect at the verb level, imposing overly rigid completion criteria or overlooking alternative interpretations, conflating interruption with non-completion, and allowing uncontrolled lexical variation.

### Overview

The dataset contains **200 premise–hypothesis examples**, organized into **50 matched four-example sets**. Each set follows a controlled 2 × 2 design that varies predicate telicity and contextual outcome information while holding the subject, lexical verb, progressive/simple-past alternation, and general scenario constant.

### Task and Labels

Given a premise describing an ongoing event and a hypothesis in the simple past, determine whether the premise entails, contradicts, or leaves unresolved the hypothesis.


### Dataset Design

Telicity is determined by the **complete predicate**, including its arguments and modifiers, rather than by the lexical verb alone. For example, *walk to the station* introduces a bounded path, whereas *walk in the station* describes an activity without an inherent endpoint.

| Group | Predicate type | Context | Label |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A | Telic | Explicit non-completion | `FALSE` |
| B | Atelic | Explicit interruption or cessation | `TRUE` |
| C | Telic | Outcome unspecified | `UNKNOWN` |
| D | Atelic | Outcome unspecified | `TRUE` |

#### Example Matched Set

The following set illustrates the construction used in the paper. All four examples share the subject *Maya* and the verb *walk*.

| Group | Premise | Hypothesis | Label |

### Limitations

#### Gold Labels and Interpretive Variation

The dataset is proposed to solve the problems discussed in the paper and support the proposed conclusion. The golden label should not be treated as uniquely correct judgments. As discussed in the paper's Limitations, aspectual interpretation and completion judgments can be inherently ambiguous in some settings and depend on lexical semantics, argument structure, discourse context, and contextual standards of completion. Binary telic–atelic categories and three-way NLI labels simplify this variation, even though the examples deliberately reduce ambiguity. The paper also notes that providing linguistic background may influence human judgments; graded acceptability judgments and distributions of human responses could complement categorical labels.

#### Scope of LLM Capability Evaluation

The dataset can be used to assess how consistently an LLM handles the specified semantic contrasts under controlled conditions. It needs further study to establish or rank general linguistic understanding, semantic competence, or reasoning ability based on the inconsistency of LLM output and labels generated by rules. High accuracy may reflect sensitivity to the selected patterns or compliance with supplied rules, while a disagreement with a gold label may require examining alternative interpretations or pragmatic inferences before attributing it to a capability failure. As emphasized in the paper's Limitations, strict semantic entailment differs from the plausible pragmatic inferences people make in ordinary language, and deviations from the benchmark’s formal criteria may reflect reliance on world knowledge or probabilistic expectations and do not, by themselves, establish a specific deficit in semantic reasoning.
